Ask what an apartment renovation costs and you will get a rate per square metre. That figure is useful for a first estimate and useless for planning a budget, because it never says what is inside it.

Below: prices by stage, a full quote for an 80 m² flat, and why two quotes for the same job can differ by half.

## In short

A turnkey apartment renovation in Limassol runs **€170–660 per m²** depending on specification. For 80 m² that is **€13,500–52,900**; a two-bedroom of 85 m² at standard specification comes to **€25,000–33,600**.

The «from €170/m²» in our service catalogue is a starting labour rate, not a total: it excludes the finishes you choose and assumes a property in sound condition.

Timeline: **6–10 weeks** without layout changes, up to 14 with them.

The main cause of gaps between quotes is not workmanship — it is scope. Waste removal, chasing for new electrics and the final clean add up to **€1,500–3,000**, and a cut-price quote simply omits them.

## The rate and the total are different numbers

**«From €170/m²»** is the floor for labour and consumables on a sound property. **The turnkey price** adds the finishes, the services and the difficulty of the details, and starts at €300/m² at standard specification.

| Specification | Turnkey per m² | 80 m² flat | 85 m² two-bed | 250 m² villa |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Economy | €170–215 | €13,500–17,100 | €13,800–17,600 | €40,000–52,000 |
| Standard | €300–410 | €24,000–32,800 | **€25,000–33,600** | €78,000–105,000 |
| Premium | €520–660 | €41,600–52,900 | €44,000–56,000 | €130,000–165,000 |

The spread inside a band comes from the state of the property: a 1985 flat with aluminium wiring and a 2015 flat of the same size differ by 15–20% at identical specification.

Economy to standard is not workmanship — it is the class of materials and the difficulty of the junctions. Standard to premium is usually bespoke joinery, large-format tile and concealed services.

The most underestimated line is **electrics**. In Limassol flats built before 2005 the wiring is almost always replaced in full, and chasing new runs into concrete is slow work. It rarely shows in an eyeballed quote and always shows on site.

## What surfaces once the walls come off

Budget with a reserve. Three situations we meet most often.

**Aluminium wiring.** Common in buildings from before the 1990s. It cannot be joined to copper directly and does not meet current rules. Full replacement adds **€800–1,800** to the electrical line.

**Damp behind bathroom tile.** Only visible at strip-out. Treatment, drying and rebuilding the substrate runs **€400–1,200**, and that is before tanking.

**Walls out of true beyond tolerance.** More than 3 cm over two metres means levelling to beads rather than skimming: **an extra €8–14 per m²** on top of normal plastering.

A sensible reserve is 10% of the quote. Not because the contractor guesses, but because nobody has seen behind that wall.

## How to spot a quote that has been cut

A quote a third below the others is usually built one of four ways.

**1. No waste removal.** Eight to fourteen cubic metres from an 80 m² flat. In Cyprus that is €1,100–2,000, arriving later as a separate invoice.

**2. No chasing.** Electrics priced as «pulling cable», with the channels in concrete treated as extra work.

**3. Understated material consumption.** Tile adhesive, primer, self-levelling compound. The difference between 4 and 6 kg per square metre is invisible in a quote and very visible in the final bill.

**4. No final clean.** Construction dust needs a professional clean: €250–450, another surprise.

Compare quotes line by line, not by the total. Forty lines against twelve does not make the second one cheaper.

## How it is paid

Tied to finished work, not to the calendar:

- **30%** on signing — date locked, materials ordered
- **30%** at first fix — strip-out, services and plastering complete
- **30%** at second fix — tiling, flooring and joinery in place
- **10%** after snagging — once the punch list is cleared

A request for more than half up front is a question about the contractor's working capital.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do apartment renovation prices vary so much?**
Because «apartment renovation» is not one product. Economy and premium differ by a factor of two at identical floor area. Compare the scope, not the headline figure.

**Can it be done for €15,000?**
Not a full cycle on 80 m². That budget covers cosmetics: painting, new floor covering, minor repairs. Full cycle starts at €13,500 economy, €24,000 standard.

**Why does the catalogue say «from €170/m²» when this says €280?**
€170/m² is the starting labour rate without the finishes, on a property in sound condition. Turnkey, with materials and services, standard specification runs €300–410/m². Both figures are honest; they answer different questions.

**Is it cheaper to do it in stages?**
No. Phased work means repeat mobilisation, repeat protection and repeat waste removal — 15–25% more in total.

**Can I buy the materials myself?**
Yes, and it is a real saving. We fit what you supply with no labour surcharge. Delivery times and defects then sit with you.

**Do I need to move out?**
For a full cycle we recommend it — water and power go off. For single-room work we phase it so you keep a working bathroom.

**Can the price change after work starts?**
Only with your signature. If something hidden turns up we stop, show you the scope and the cost, and you decide.
