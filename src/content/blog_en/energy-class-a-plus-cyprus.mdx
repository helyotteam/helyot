In Cyprus the summer electricity bill is a line item in the household budget. In an older house the air conditioning runs almost continuously from June to September, and €1,000 a month surprises nobody.

The NZEB standard changes that arithmetic. Here is what it requires, what it costs and what it genuinely returns.

## In short

Every new house in Cyprus must meet class **A+** under the Near-Zero Energy Buildings standard. It is a condition of getting a permit, not an aspiration.

Meeting it adds **8–14% to the build cost** and returns **€3,000–5,000 a year** on electricity. Over ten years, €30,000–50,000.

## What the standard requires

| Requirement | NZEB level | What it used to be |
|---|---|---|
| Wall insulation | 80–100 mm | 30–50 mm or none |
| Solar panels | from 4.17–6 kW | not required |
| Windows | thermal, low heat transfer | single-cavity units |
| Roof | insulation plus reflective coating | basic waterproofing |
| Energy calculation | mandatory in the design | not required |

## What it saves

Figures for a 200 m² house on the Limassol coast.

| Season | Old house (class C) | New house (A+) | Monthly saving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summer, June–August | €800–1,200 | €200–300 | €600–900 |
| Winter, December–February | €300–500 | €100–150 | €200–350 |
| Shoulder season | €150–250 | €50–80 | €100–170 |

**Annual saving: €3,000–5,000.**

Most of the difference is summer. Insulated walls do not release the day's heat back into the rooms at night, and the panels cover the daytime peak when the air conditioning is working hardest — exactly when the sun is strongest. That overlap between generation and demand is what makes solar pay in Cyprus.

## What it costs to meet

For a 200 m² house:

- Wall insulation at 80–100 mm: **€12,000–18,000**
- 5 kW solar array installed: **€5,000–8,000**
- Thermal windows over standard units: **plus €8,000–14,000**
- Roof insulation and reflective coating: **€4,000–7,000**
- Calculation and documentation: **€800–1,500**

That is **€30,000–48,000** against a €480,000 build — the 8–14%.

At €3,000–5,000 saved a year the outlay returns in 7–12 years. After that it is clean saving, plus a higher resale value: an A+ certificate is an argument for both buyers and tenants.

## Insulation or panels — which matters more

Insulation. Panels generate energy; insulation removes the need for it, around the clock, with no servicing and no degradation.

Panels lose roughly 0.5% of output a year and need washing in the dusty season. An insulated wall is exactly as insulated in twenty years.

The right order is the building envelope first — walls, roof, windows. Panels after.

## How to check it was actually done

1. **The Energy Performance Certificate** showing class A+. Ask for the document, not a verbal assurance.
2. **Insulation thickness in the specification**, not "insulated to standard".
3. **Panel output in kW** and the inverter make.
4. **The window U-value** on the product data sheet.

A house without an A+ certificate will not get a permit, but execution varies within the class. The specification shows what you actually got.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I skip NZEB and build cheaper?**
No. It is a condition of the building permit.

**Do panels pay back in Cyprus?**
Faster than almost anywhere in Europe: 4–6 years for a domestic system with high daytime consumption.

**What does A+ do for resale?**
A higher price and a faster sale — the buyer can see the running costs in advance.

**Does this work on renovating an older house?**
Partly. Insulation and windows deliver most of the effect.
