A Cyprus electricity bill in summer is its own budget line. In an uninsulated house the air conditioning runs almost continuously from June to September, and €700–1,200 a month surprises nobody.

The state part-funds the work that brings that figure down. But the money follows a document, not an intention — the energy audit report. Here is what it shows, what it costs, and where applications get stuck.

## In short

An audit costs **€300–600 for a flat** and **€600–1,200 for a house**. It is mandatory for a grant application and partly reimbursed on approval.

The Energy Service's SAVE programme covers up to **€10,000** per property. Assessment takes **4–8 weeks**; the full cycle from audit to payment is usually **4–7 months**.

The commonest cause of refusal is work started before approval. Nothing is paid retrospectively.

## What an audit is

A certified assessor establishes where the building loses energy and what that costs the owner each year. The output is a report with a class from A to G and a list of measures with payback for each.

It is not a formality. The report decides which works are eligible at all and at what level.

## What the survey covers

| Stage | What is done | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Visual inspection | facade, roof, windows, doors, junctions | visible defects in the envelope |
| Thermal imaging | survey of the building envelope | cold bridges, hidden losses |
| Consumption analysis | 12 months of bills | the real seasonal profile |
| Services check | cooling, water heating, ventilation | efficiency and wear of equipment |
| Class calculation | European methodology | current class and the room to improve |

Thermal imaging in Cyprus is most useful **in the heat**, not in winter: the summer differential between outside and inside is larger and the hot spots read clearly. A July or August survey gives a more useful picture than a January one.

## What it costs

| Property | Audit fee |
|---|---|
| Flat up to 100 m² | €300–450 |
| Flat over 100 m² | €400–600 |
| House up to 200 m² | €600–900 |
| House over 200 m² | €900–1,200 |

On approval the audit cost is partly reimbursed. If the application is refused it is not, so it pays to assess the odds first.

## What the SAVE programme funds

Funded by the Cyprus Energy Service. The main lines:

| Work | Grant ceiling |
|---|---|
| Roof insulation | up to €2,000 |
| Wall insulation | up to €3,000 |
| Heat pump | up to €3,500 |
| Solar water heater | up to €1,500 |
| Overall ceiling per property | **up to €10,000** |

Amounts and conditions are revised periodically, so confirm the current figures with the Energy Service at the point of application — these are orders of magnitude.

## What each measure returns

A 200 m² house on the Limassol coast, annual saving.

| Measure | Cost | Saving per year | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roof insulation, 80–100 mm | €4,000–7,000 | €700–1,100 | 5–8 years |
| External wall insulation | €12,000–18,000 | €1,200–1,900 | 8–12 years |
| Thermal windows | €8,000–14,000 | €600–1,000 | 10–14 years |
| Heat pump | €4,500–8,000 | €900–1,500 | 4–7 years |
| Solar panels, 5 kW | €5,000–8,000 | €1,100–1,700 | 4–6 years |

The roof is almost always first for return: in Cyprus that is where the summer load comes through. Wall insulation gives more in absolute terms and takes longer to pay back.

Requirements for new build are stricter — see [energy class A+ in Cyprus](/en/blog/energy-class-a-plus-cyprus).

## The sequence

1. **Audit application** through the Energy Service portal
2. **Survey** by a certified assessor, 1–2 days on site
3. **Report** with class and measures, 1–2 weeks
4. **Grant application** with the full document pack
5. **Approval**, 4–8 weeks
6. **Works** by a certified contractor — only after approval
7. **Inspection and payment**, 4–8 weeks after completion

Full cycle: **4–7 months**. Plan the works around that, not around the season.

## Why applications fail

**Work started before approval.** The commonest and most painful. Nothing is funded retrospectively, however well the work was done and however complete the paperwork.

**Uncertified contractor.** The installer must be on the register. Work by an uncertified crew is not accepted for reimbursement.

**Divergence from the report.** If the audit specifies 100 mm of roof insulation and 60 mm goes on, payment is cut or refused outright.

**Incomplete pack.** Title, electricity bills, technical certificate, contract, completion records. One missing document pushes the file into another round.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a flat get a grant, or only a house?**
Flats are eligible. But work touching common property — roof, facade — needs the management company's and neighbours' consent.

**Is an audit mandatory if I just want to insulate the roof?**
For work at your own cost, no. For a grant, yes — no report, no application.

**How long is a report valid?**
Generally accepted within a year of issue. Leave it longer and the audit has to be repeated.

**What if the house is old and the class is poor?**
That helps rather than hurts: the lower the starting class, the larger the improvement each measure delivers and the stronger the application looks.

**Can grant work be combined with an ordinary renovation?**
Yes, and it is cheaper that way — scaffolding, protection and waste removal are paid for once. What matters is separating the funded scope from the self-funded scope in the paperwork.
