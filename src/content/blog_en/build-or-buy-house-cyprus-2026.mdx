You find the perfect plot with a sea view. You make an offer. You pay a deposit. A month later your lawyer tells you the plot is mortgaged to a bank and the title will take five years. Or worse: the house was built badly, the foundation is cracking, and the electricity bill reads like a second mortgage.

These are not scare stories. They are what happens to buyers in Cyprus every year. Below is a full account of the risks and costs you will meet when buying a plot or a finished house.

## In short

In 2026, building a quality house in Cyprus turnkey — meeting the mandatory energy-efficiency rules (class A+, NZEB) — costs **€2,000–3,200 per m²**, which is 25–42% less than buying a finished villa. A basic shell without finishes can start at €1,700, but budget from €2,000 per m² for somewhere you can actually live to modern standards.

First-home buyers get a reduced VAT rate of 5% on the first 130 m² (up to €350,000). New houses must meet energy class A+, which saves €3,000–5,000 a year on electricity. Stamp duty was abolished on 1 January 2026.

The main risk when buying finished property is a mortgaged title and a long wait for registration. Build on your own plot and the title is yours as soon as the work is done.

## Why a finished villa can cost more than it looks

Buying a finished house means paying the developer's margin and a design you did not choose — with no idea what is behind the finishes.

What we find most often:

- foundations poured badly, with up to 20% saved on concrete;
- no basement waterproofing;
- wiring never sized for modern loads;
- low-insulation windows, and electricity bills two to three times higher.

Building means you control every layer, from the foundation bed to the final coat.

### The hidden €50,000: sloping plots

Cyprus is hills. Paphos especially (Peyia, Tala) and Limassol (Agios Athanasios, Panthea). Plots with a sea view almost always slope, and nobody puts the retaining wall in the listing.

| Change in level | Retaining wall cost |
|---|---|
| 2–3 metres | €15,000–25,000 |
| 5 metres and more | €40,000–80,000 |
| Rocky ground | add €10,000–20,000 for drilling |

**How to check before you buy:** order a topographic survey for €500–1,000. It shows the real change in level and lets you price the slope work before you sign anything.

### Bank charges and Title Deeds

Many developers mortgage the land and the buildings on it. When you buy a finished villa you often receive a sale contract rather than a Title Deed, and the bank blocks the title until the developer clears the loan.

In practice: you buy for €700,000, the developer owes the bank €500,000, the bank will not release the title until the debt is settled — and you wait years.

Build on a plot with a clean title and the title is issued as soon as construction finishes.

> Always ask the seller for a five-day search from the Land Registry. It shows every encumbrance before the deal.

### Energy class A+ and what it actually saves

Since 2024–2026 every new house must meet the Near-Zero Energy Buildings (NZEB) standard: wall insulation from 80–100 mm, solar panels from 4.17–6 kW, and thermal windows with low heat transfer.

| Season | Old house (class C) | New house (class A+) | Monthly saving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summer, June–August | €800–1,200 | €200–300 | €600–900 |
| Winter, December–February | €300–500 | €100–150 | €200–350 |
| Shoulder season | €150–250 | €50–80 | €100–170 |

That is €3,000–5,000 a year. Over ten years, €30,000–50,000.

## Taxes and fees estate agents do not mention

### Transfer Fees

A progressive tax when you buy a plot from a private seller. A 50% discount applies to all buyers.

| Plot value | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to €85,000 | 3% |
| €85,001 – €170,000 | 5% |
| Above €170,000 | 8% |

For a €250,000 plot: €85,000 × 3% = €2,550, plus €85,000 × 5% = €4,250, plus €80,000 × 8% = €6,400. That is €13,200, and **€6,600** after the 50% discount.

### Tax on the land and tax on the build are different things

This is where most of the confusion sits, so let us separate it.

**On the land purchase** you pay one of two, and they are mutually exclusive:

- the plot is sold with VAT (by a developer or a company) — Transfer Fees are 0%;
- the plot is sold without VAT (by a private seller) — Transfer Fees apply, with the 50% discount.

**On the construction work** you pay VAT on the contract: 5% on the first 130 m² for a first home (up to €350,000) and 19% on the rest. This applies regardless of how the plot was bought.

### Stamp duty abolished from 1 January 2026

Law 19/1963 was repealed by Law No. 239(I)/2025. No stamp duty is payable on contracts signed from 1 January 2026. It used to be around €800–1,000 on a €450,000 house.

### Annual sewerage board fee

Calculated on the assessed value, not the market value, at 0.05–0.25%. For a house assessed at €300,000 that is €150–400 a year.

## What separates professionals from amateurs

### Concrete at +40 °C

A Cyprus summer is brutal for concrete. Above +32 °C water evaporates faster than the cement can hydrate, and the concrete loses 30–40% of its design strength.

How professionals work around it:

- plasticisers to slow the set in heat;
- night pours, between 05:00 and 09:00;
- temperature control — no higher than +32 °C at the moment of placing;
- water curing three times a day for seven days after the pour;
- sulphate-resistant cement for coastal sites.

**Ask your contractor:** "How do you pour concrete in August?" If the answer includes plasticisers and night pours, you are talking to professionals.

## Permits: realistic timelines for 2026

The Ippodamos system promises 40–80 working days. Reality is different: 8,500 cases inherited from the reform, roughly 1,000 new applications a month in Limassol alone, and up to half of applications returned for correction.

Realistic timelines: best case 3–6 months, typical 8–12 months, worst case years.

## Four kinds of people who should not build

**Moving in a hurry.** The full cycle runs 18–28 months. If you need it fast, buy finished.

**Budget with nothing spare.** Allow at least 25–30% on top: ground survey, retaining walls, material price rises.

**Remote investor.** Work only with a company that gives you video reports and site cameras.

**Buyer of a cheap plot.** Never buy without a ground survey and a check on utilities.

From €15,000 at a 2–3 metre change in level to €80,000 at 5 metres and above.
