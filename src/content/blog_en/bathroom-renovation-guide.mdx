A bathroom is the smallest room in the property and the one most likely to be rebuilt twice. It carries every service, every wet junction and the least tolerance for a shortcut — and unlike a bedroom, nothing in it can be corrected later without demolition.

What it costs, how long it takes, and which layer decides whether the room lasts ten years or two.

## In short

A full bathroom rebuild in Limassol runs **€2,000–3,500** at economy, **€4,000–6,500** standard, **€7,000–14,000** premium for a typical 4–6 m² room.

Timeline is **2–3 weeks** for a single bathroom, longer only if the layout moves.

The layer that decides the outcome is **tanking**: €300–600 done properly, €6,000–15,000 to put right after a leak, because the floor below usually comes into it.

## What it costs

| Specification | 4–6 m² room | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Economy | €2,000–3,500 | strip-out, tanking, standard tile, basic fittings |
| Standard | €4,000–6,500 | full services renewal, mid-range tile and sanitaryware, shower screen |
| Premium | €7,000–14,000 | large-format tile, concealed cisterns and valves, bespoke vanity, underfloor heating |

Sanitaryware and tile you choose yourself sit inside those bands when we buy them, or outside if you buy them.

The jump from economy to standard is mostly services: economy keeps the existing pipework where it is sound, standard replaces it. That is also why economy is only honest in a property where the services are actually sound.

## Tanking is the whole job

Everything else in a bathroom can be replaced without breaking anything. Tanking cannot — it sits under the tile, and getting to it means taking the room apart.

**What correct looks like:** a system to **EN 14891**, taken up the walls at least 200 mm and to full height in the shower, with taped and sealed corners, junctions and pipe penetrations. Tested before tiling.

**What cheap looks like:** a coat of something along the joints, tile straight over it.

The difference is €300–600 at the time. The consequence is a leak into the flat below at year one or two, and €6,000–15,000 to make good — the bathroom, plus a ceiling that is not yours.

Ask for photographs of the tanking before tile goes on. It is the only stage that cannot be inspected afterwards, so it is the only stage that has to be photographed.

## Ventilation is the second decision

A Cyprus bathroom without working extract grows mould, particularly through the humid coastal winter and particularly in a property that is let, where several people shower daily.

Working extract means a fan on a humidity sensor with a run-on timer, a gap under the door for makeup air, and a duct that actually reaches outside rather than into a void.

Cost at renovation: €150–300. Cost of fighting mould afterwards: €2,000–5,000, and it returns every season until the airflow is fixed. Mould is nearly always a ventilation fault, not a wall fault.

## Materials that survive here

| Element | What fails | What works |
|---|---|---|
| Grout | cementitious | epoxy |
| Floor | laminate, engineered timber | porcelain, SPC |
| Ironmongery near the coast | thin chrome, plated steel | stainless A4 |
| Paint above tile | standard emulsion | moisture-resistant, washable |
| Shower screen fittings | mild steel | stainless, coated aluminium |

Epoxy grout costs three to four times cementitious and is the single cheapest upgrade in the room: it does not stain, does not blacken and does not need replacing at year three.

## Timeline

| Stage | Duration |
|---|---|
| Strip-out | 1–2 days |
| First fix, plumbing and electrics | 2–3 days |
| Levelling and tanking, including cure | 3–4 days |
| Tiling | 3–5 days |
| Second fix and fittings | 2–3 days |
| Sealing and snagging | 1–2 days |

Roughly **2–3 weeks**. The tanking cure is not compressible: tiling over an uncured membrane is how a correct specification still fails.

## When consent is needed

Replacing a bathroom in place — no. Moving the room, moving the soil stack, or taking down a wall to enlarge it — usually yes. What needs consent and what does not is set out in [renovation permits in Cyprus](/en/blog/renovation-permits-cyprus-guide).

## Frequently asked questions

**How long can we be without the bathroom?**
Two to three weeks for a full rebuild. In a single-bathroom property we phase the work so the room is usable overnight for most of it, but there are three or four days where it is not.

**Can the existing tile be tiled over?**
Technically sometimes, in practice rarely worth it. You inherit whatever is beneath, including the tanking you cannot see, and lose 15–20 mm of floor height.

**What does moving the toilet cost?**
€400–1,200 depending on how far and whether the fall to the stack can be maintained. Beyond about two metres it usually needs a macerator or a raised floor.

**Is underfloor heating worth it in Cyprus?**
In a bathroom, often yes — it is the one room where winter tile is genuinely unpleasant. €400–900 for a 5 m² room, and it runs only a few months a year.

**How do I know the tanking was done properly?**
You cannot, once it is tiled. Require photographs of the completed membrane and the flood test as part of handover.
