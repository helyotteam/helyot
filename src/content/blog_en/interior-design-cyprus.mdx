A design package looks like the line you could cut. In practice it usually pays for itself before the first crew walks in: decisions made on paper cost a tenth of decisions made against a wall.

What it costs, which styles hold up in this climate, and what to specify if the property will be let.

## In short

A design package runs **€25–45 per m²** — for an 80 m² flat, €2,000–3,600. It takes **3–6 weeks** from survey to complete drawings.

It pays for itself two ways: it removes the reworks that typically eat **8–15% of a renovation budget**, and it lets materials be bought in one order instead of topped up mid-job.

For a rental, the style affects the rate more than the finishing budget does: in Limassol the gap between a considered interior and an accidental one is **20–35% of monthly income**.

## What a package contains

| Drawing | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Measured survey | actual dimensions with every deviation | without it, quotes lie |
| Layout options | 2–3 arrangements | chosen before any wall comes down |
| 3D visuals | per room | you see the result before paying for it |
| Wall elevations | tile setting-out, sockets, switches | removes «we ran short here» |
| Electrical plan | every point located | chased once, not twice |
| Floor and ceiling plans | finishes, levels, lighting | sets door heights |
| Material schedule | references, quantities, suppliers | one order, one batch |

The schedule is the most underrated sheet. Without it, materials get topped up in batches — and tile from a second batch is a different shade, timber a different cut.

## What it costs

| Package | Contents | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Layout | survey, layout, schedule | €12–18/m² |
| Standard | plus 3D per room, elevations | €25–35/m² |
| Full | plus site supervision and supplier selection | €38–45/m² |

For an 80 m² flat: **€960–1,440** layout, **€2,000–2,800** standard, **€3,000–3,600** full.

Site supervision in the full package means the designer visits as work proceeds. On complex jobs with concealed services and bespoke joinery it earns its keep: one setting-out error caught in time costs more than the whole supervision fee.

## Three styles that work in Cyprus

### Contemporary Mediterranean

**For:** villas, ground floors with terraces, houses in the old town.

Natural stone or large-format porcelain in a limestone read, arched openings, lime finishes, linen and cotton, wrought detail. The style belongs to the island and does not date: in ten years it still looks deliberate.

**Budget note:** natural stone costs two to three times porcelain and needs re-sealing every 3–5 years. Porcelain in a limestone finish gives the same image with no maintenance.

### Contemporary minimalism

**For:** city-centre flats, Marina apartments, newer developments.

Clean lines, a neutral palette, concealed storage. The most predictable choice for letting: it photographs well and offends nobody.

**The catch:** minimalism does not forgive walls out of true. Concealed doors, shadow gaps and junctions without architrave need a higher class of plastering — an extra €8–14 per m² on first fix.

### Japandi and Scandinavian

**For:** one to three-bed flats, properties aimed at European expats and long lets.

Pale timber, natural textures, a quiet palette, quality over quantity. It has taken hold in Limassol over recent years and reads well to corporate tenants.

**Climate limit:** pale solid timber flooring in a flat with full-height glazing bleaches. Either UV film on the glass, or SPC with a lightfast decor.

## What to specify for a rental

| Style | Short let | Long let | Who books it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mediterranean | high rate | medium | tourists, families |
| Minimalism | high | high | broad audience |
| Japandi / Scandinavian | medium | high | expats, corporate |

For short lets, **Mediterranean and minimalism** work hardest — they photograph better, and a booking decision is made from photographs in seconds.

For long lets **Japandi** wins: quieter, less «designed», and it does not tire a tenant after six months.

## What the climate dictates

**Direct sun eight months a year.** Textiles, art and pale timber bleach beside full-height glazing. Budget UV film or specify lightfast materials.

**Salt on the coast.** Within roughly a kilometre of the sea, ordinary ironmongery and chrome corrode in 2–4 years. Stainless or powder-coated only.

**Winter humidity.** In uninsulated buildings condensation settles in the corners of external walls. Dark paint and wallpaper show it first — either insulate, or finish pale and breathable.

**Dust.** The kalima season brings a fine dust that shows on gloss. Matt and micro-textured surfaces win on maintenance.

## How the package pays for itself

A standard package on an 80 m² flat costs around €2,400. What it prevents:

| Avoided | Typical cost of the error |
|---|---|
| Moving sockets after plastering | €300–700 |
| Re-setting-out tile | €600–1,500 |
| Replacing doors after floor build-up | €800–2,000 |
| Topping up tile from another batch | €400–1,200 |
| Redoing lighting after the ceiling | €500–1,400 |

Two of those five and the package is level. In practice there are usually more.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can the design stage be skipped?**
On cosmetic work, yes. On a full cycle with layout changes and new services, the drawings cost less than not having them.

**How long does it take?**
3–6 weeks: a week for survey and layout, two or three for visuals and revisions, a week for working drawings and the schedule.

**How many revisions are included?**
A sound contract allows two rounds on layout and two on visuals. Beyond that it is chargeable, and fairly so: endless revisions mean the brief was never settled.

**Must the designer be the same firm that builds?**
No, but there are fewer gaps that way. When designer and builder are separate companies, contested details turn into correspondence rather than decisions.

**What if I already have drawings from another designer?**
We build to them. We check them against the property and flag anything that will not land — before work starts, not during.
