The permit is the least predictable stage of building in Cyprus. Not because the rules are complicated, but because the system is overloaded and half of all applications come back for correction.

## In short

The official target in the **Ippodamos** system is 40–80 working days. Real timelines: **3–6 months** at best, **8–12 months** typically, and years in difficult cases.

The main cause of delay is not the queue — it is the quality of the submission. Up to half of applications are returned, and each return adds 4–10 weeks.

## Why the promise and the reality differ

The reform moved approvals into the digital Ippodamos system, and **8,500 inherited cases** moved with it. Limassol alone receives roughly **1,000 new applications a month**.

Approval also involves more than the planning office: the fire service, the water board, the electricity authority and the roads department all sign off. Each moves at its own pace, and the application waits for the slowest.

## A realistic schedule

| Stage | Duration |
|---|---|
| Preparing the design package | 4–10 weeks |
| Utility authority approvals | 4–12 weeks |
| Municipal review | 8–20 weeks |
| Corrections, if returned | 4–10 weeks per round |

Plan for **8–12 months** from submission to permit, and build your plot purchase and contractor timeline around that figure rather than the advertised 40 days.

## What gets sent back most often

**Breaching the building coefficients.** Every plot has density, coverage and height coefficients. A design that exceeds any of them by even one per cent is returned in full.

**Setbacks from boundaries.** Distances to neighbouring plots and to the road are regulated. Half a metre out means another round.

**No NZEB calculation.** With class A+ now mandatory, the energy performance calculation is part of the package. Without it the application is not accepted.

**Incomplete utility drawings.** Water, drainage and electrical schemes must be agreed before submission, not after.

**Drawings that disagree with each other.** Plan, sections and elevations must match dimensionally. It sounds obvious, and it is where most returns originate.

## What to do while you wait

Waiting for a permit is not a pause. In those months you can:

- run the ground survey and find out what you are building on;
- order the topographic survey and price [retaining walls](/en/blog/retaining-walls-cyprus) if the plot slopes;
- select and approve finishes — some items carry 8–12 week lead times;
- sign the contract and fix the price before materials move.

Used properly, the wait shortens the overall project by 2–3 months.

## When no permit is needed

Not every job requires one. Usually no permit for:

- internal finishes with no change to the layout;
- replacing windows in existing openings;
- roof repair without changing the geometry;
- landscaping with no permanent structures.

Always required for: new build, extensions, facade changes, alterations to load-bearing structure, swimming pools, and retaining walls above the set height.

If in doubt, ask the municipality for written confirmation. A verbal "that's fine" is worth nothing when an enforcement notice arrives.

## How to shorten it

1. **Submit a complete package first time.** One return costs more than two weeks spent completing it.
2. **Use an architect who is an ETEK member.** They know the specific municipality's requirements.
3. **Check the coefficients before designing**, not after. Redesigning takes months.
4. **Avoid submitting in December and August** — holiday season stretches the review.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I start building before the permit?**
No. Fines and a demolition order are real outcomes, and retrospective legalisation is more expensive and not guaranteed.

**Who submits — me or the contractor?**
The application is in your name, but the contractor or architect prepares and files it. We carry our projects through to permit.

**What is the fee?**
€1,500–4,000 depending on area and municipality, paid at cost with the receipt.

**Is there a fast-track?**
Not officially. The only method that works is getting the submission right first time.
